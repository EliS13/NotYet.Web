Privacy policy

Last updated: October 1, 2026

This covers the Chrome extension Not yet. It keeps YouTube and any other sites you choose closed until your list is done, closes them at bedtime, and keeps your calendar next to your homework. Here is everything it does with your information.

  • Stays in your browserHabits, tasks, notes and settings are saved in Chrome, on your computer.
  • Google is optionalIf you sign in, your calendars and tasks go only between your browser and Google.

What it stores

Your habits, tasks, events you add, sticky notes, timer history and settings. All of it is saved in your own browser using Chrome's extension storage. Unless you sign in with Google (below), none of it leaves your computer, and nobody else can see it, including the developer.

What it fetches

If you add a calendar's secret link, the extension downloads that calendar directly from your calendar provider (for example Google Calendar, Outlook or iCloud) so it can show your events and turn tagged ones into tasks. The links you paste in are stored only in your browser. It asks for permission to read each link's site when you add it.

Apart from that and Google Calendar (below), nothing is fetched. There are no analytics, ads or trackers, and no servers of the developer's.

Focus sounds, if you turn them on, are made on your computer while a task timer runs. Nothing is downloaded, recorded or sent.

If you sign in with Google

Signing in is optional. If you do, the extension talks to Google Calendar directly from your browser, with the permissions you approve on Google's sign-in screen: seeing your list of calendars, and seeing, adding, changing and deleting events. It uses them only to show your events here and to save the events you add or change here. Your calendar goes only between your browser and Google; it never goes to the developer or anyone else. The pass Google gives the extension for this is kept in memory only and is gone when you close the browser.

To stop, choose Remove next to the account in Settings → Connections → Google, or remove Not yet. from the third-party connections in your Google Account.

Not yet.'s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

If you connect other apps

Connections are optional. If you connect Todoist, Google Tasks, Microsoft To Do or Canvas, the extension reads your unfinished tasks that have a due date straight from that app, and tells the app when you tick or untick one of them here. It uses them only to show those tasks in your list. They go only between your browser and that app; they never go to the developer or anyone else. The token or sign-in that lets it do this stays in your browser, and Settings → Connections → Disconnect removes it (for Google, Remove next to the account). Google Tasks comes with your Google sign-in: Google's screen asks for it together with your calendars, and you can leave it unticked.

What it does on the sites it closes

On YouTube, and on any other site you switch on, the extension checks the address of the page you're on so it can show the lock screen or close the site at bedtime. On YouTube it also reads the length of the video you start on a school night. It asks Chrome for access to each extra site only when you switch that site on. It doesn't record or send your browsing or viewing history anywhere.

Deleting your data

Settings → Backup → Delete everything erases everything saved in the extension. Removing the extension from Chrome does the same. Removing a Google account in Settings → Connections → Google ends the extension's access to it; events in your Google Calendar stay there.
